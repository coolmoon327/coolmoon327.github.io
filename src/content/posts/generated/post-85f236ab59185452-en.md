## KU

- Do not exchange currency at the airport; use an ATM instead
  - Airports allow cash-for-cash exchanges, but it's expensive
    - 1 : 2.7
  - A Visa card can be used to withdraw cash at an ATM; UnionPay also works at some locations
    - 1 : 1.95
    - A fee of 12; withdraw a larger amount in one transaction
    - Only Class I bank cards support cross-border payments
  - A Visa card is preferable; carrying too much cash is not recommended
    - You can use your parents' card for payments
    - Alipay is supported, but it must be switched to the local version
  - You can pay in USD and receive change in Dirhams, but you'll lose out on the exchange rate
    - Calculated based on  3.65 
- Food
  - The pasta counter is recommended
  - Drinks are very sweet; KARAK is recommended
  - Vegetables and fruits are expensive
  - The food may be fine initially, but the heavy use of spices can become difficult to tolerate
  - Chinese food is very expensive and tastes bad (60  Dirhams)
    - The school offers reasonably good Chinese boxed meals through a group order (20 dirhams)
  - Hamburgers are cheap (20-30  Dirhams,  15  Dirhams on campus), but there aren't many fries, and you'll get tired of them if you eat too much
  - Expenses are  2-3  times those in China, with small restaurants costing around 40-50 per meal
    - Two dirhams for a bottle of water
    - The cost of living is roughly twice that of Chengdu, based on the numbers.
- Male and female dormitories are located in different campuses.
  - The dormitories are suite-style.
    - Each suite has four rooms, each with a bed, a desk, and an air conditioner.
  - Khalifa University (main building) is in another campus.
    - There are four buildings, divided by functional areas.
    - Fewer people, more desks and chairs.
  - All three campuses are located within the city center.
  - Campus shuttles run every morning at regular intervals.
    - Recitation of the Quran takes place on the shuttle; please do not disturb.
  - The indoor air conditioning is very strong (14  degrees). When turned off, it's around  20  degrees, so you still need to bring extra clothes.
    - Wear shirts outdoors and jackets indoors.
    - Pay attention to dress code requirements; shorts are not allowed indoors.
  - Bring some medicine as you may get sick.
- Public transportation is underdeveloped.
  - Only buses are available.
    - 2 Dirham
    - A bus card is required; coins are not accepted. Activate and top up the card at designated stations.
    - It's slow, stopping frequently along the way.
  - Taxi fares are four to five times higher than in China.
    - Prefer paying by bank card; when paying cash, a large-denomination note may be kept as the full fare.
  - Public security is decent; walking at night is fine.
    - However, protect your belongings: **money can be stolen on buses.**
- Sports venues are basically empty.
  - Locals only play football.

## Dubai

- You can take a taxi or bus there
  - The bus costs 25 dirhams, runs from 6:00 a.m. to 1:00 a.m., and takes about 90 minutes
- Metro
  - Purchase a metro card for 25  Dirhams (with a 7  Dirham activation fee)
  - The metro is expensive, but the alternatives cost even more.
  - The main attractions are on the Red Line and generally do not require transfers.
    - If you can't find a place, ask around—locals are very friendly.
    - Locals have no problem speaking English.
  - Subway cards and bus cards are not interchangeable.
  - There's a subway map with Chinese annotations on Xiaohongshu.
- Palm Jumeirah
  - Take the Red Line to DMCC, transfer to the Orange Line to Palm Gateway, then take the monorail to the second stop.
  - Buy the observation-deck ticket on Meituan and choose the highest-floor option, which costs an additional 100 RMB.
  - The Atlantis aquarium offers free admission on your birthday; allow a full day for the visit.
- Burj Al Arab
  - Take the Orange Line to the end, then walk a short distance

## Special

- Locals wear white robes
  - The original note describes them as nobles; wearing a headband is formal attire
  - Only local women wear black robes
  - About 60% are Indian or Pakistani migrant workers
    - Most bus passengers are Indian workers
    - Locals drive themselves
- Do not sit in the front seats of buses and subways; they are reserved for women and families (children).
  - Be mindful of some gold card reserved seats.
- Observe “ladies first”; be mindful that some women may prefer not to share an elevator.
- Drinking alcohol in public places is prohibited.
  - No smoking or drinking.
- You may need to boil water first in the dormitory.
  - Female dormitories require boiling water, while male dormitories do not seem to.
- Medicines with instruction manuals are allowed.
- It is generally very safe; work ends at 5 PM, and by 6 PM, most people have left.
  - 70% Shops may stay open until early morning.
  - Many people are still shopping after 10 PM
- As long as it's not a formal occasion, you can dress casually in private
- Sunset is early in winter; some scenic areas close when the sun sets (at 5 PM)
