# Constrained Policy Optimization

---

> **A brief introduction to CMDPs:** Building on an MDP, a CMDP defines a set of constraint-return functions. Like the MDP's return function, the CMDP discounts every constraint return and requires each discounted value under a policy to remain below its corresponding threshold. The optimization objective remains the MDP's maximization of discounted return.
>
> **Constrained policy optimization:** Policy-search algorithms can effectively handle high-dimensional or continuous MDPs. In essence, they search within a neighborhood of the current policy for one with a better objective value. In a CMDP, policy optimization must evaluate whether every newly discovered policy satisfies the constraints, which is clearly difficult.

## I. Research Background

Existing reinforcement-learning algorithms focus on convergence performance and have been shown to permit agents to explore with complete freedom during training. In real-world training, this can create **safety problems**. For example, a drone's unrestricted exploration may damage expensive equipment, while a factory robot arm may pose a safety threat to nearby workers during training.

It is natural to translate safety problems into **constraints** and model them using a **constrained Markov decision process** (CMDP). Before this paper, however, no continuous CMDP algorithm had been proposed that guaranteed constraint satisfaction **during training**.

Compared with earlier work, CPO was the first policy-search algorithm to keep an agent within the constraints throughout training—albeit approximately—while supporting arbitrary policy classes, including neural networks.

## II. Target Problem

In a CMDP, a policy-gradient algorithm adds constraints on the discounted constraint-return values. This requires checking feasibility whenever a new policy is found, but updating such an optimization problem in a continuous, high-dimensional space is **difficult**. One obvious approach is to calculate policy updates directly from samples, but this requires off-policy evaluation, which is highly challenging.

CPO's target problem is to design a reliable policy-gradient algorithm for CMDPs that can perform **policy updates in high-dimensional spaces**. It avoids the constraint-estimation problem by using a more readily estimated **approximation function**.

Policy-sampling algorithms also often suffer from **performance collapse**, generally because the update step size is poorly chosen. CPO proposes a trust-region method similar to TRPO. While using an approximation function to **simplify the objective**, it also guarantees **monotonic improvement throughout the optimization process**.

## III. Core Method

**Constrained Policy Optimization (CPO)** is a trust-region method for CMDPs. It uses an easily estimated **approximation function**. Such functions are commonly updated by deriving a lower bound and then raising that bound during optimization, thereby optimizing the original problem. The question therefore becomes: **how can we obtain a lower bound and optimize it?**

### b. Trust-Region Method

The objective maximizes the mean advantage function, precisely the earlier lower-bound approximation of the difference between the new and old policies. By controlling the distance between the policies, CPO ensures that the new policy comes from the trust region. Samples from the old policy can then approximate the new policy effectively, guaranteeing **monotonic performance improvement**.

### d. Approximate Solution of the CPO Update

A first-order expansion of the expected advantage produces the **policy gradient**, while a second-order expansion of the KL divergence produces the **Fisher information matrix (FIM)**.
