> [Notice on the First 2023 Selection Round for the School of Information and Communication Engineering's Innovative International Talent Training Program in AI and IoT]
>
> Students interested in applying for a **joint doctoral program at a leading overseas university** or **doctoral study at an overseas university** are invited to learn more.
> The School of Information and Communication Engineering is cooperating with Delft University of Technology in the Netherlands (ranked 57th in the QS World University Rankings at the time). The school conducts selection, and the China Scholarship Council fully funds this new round of the innovative-talent program.
>
> Program advantages:
> 1. Independent selection—the school and university directly select candidates. A fixed number of places is available each year, offering strong further-study and learning opportunities to outstanding graduating master's students and current doctoral students.
> 2. Dual-supervisor system—the joint doctoral program uses two supervisors, who optimize the training mechanism according to the curriculum and each student's circumstances.
>
> An online program briefing was scheduled for 16:00 on Thursday, November 17, 2022, to introduce the program and answer questions.
> Program overview and briefing arrangements: <https://www.sice.uestc.edu.cn/info/1144/10463.htm>
>
> The program had ample places, and interested students were welcome to attend.

## Program Content and Advantages

- The Delft program waives tuition; the CSC covers living expenses and airfare.
  - It uses the CSC high-level program.
  - At the time of these notes, all applications submitted through the program had been approved by the CSC.
  - Many Delft programs did not accept CSC funding, but this program had an agreement and was unaffected.
  - Delft covered the €20,000–€40,000 bench fee.
- TOEFL 100, with 21 in each section; a supervisor could request a language-requirement waiver at admission.
  - The language requirement did not need to be met for the December application, but had to be met by March of the following year.
  - With a strong enough CV, a slightly lower English score could be discussed.
- A typical independent AI application might select one candidate from 100–150 applicants, but the Delft program's chances were described as very high, with seven or eight doctoral places and another seven or eight joint-training places.
  - There were, in practice, many places.
- Graduation emphasized publication quality rather than quantity. Systems work was expected to produce two top-conference papers, each of which could take more than a year.
- With a sufficiently strong CV, the estimated chance of admission was 70%–80%; interview preparation was the main concern.
  - Interviews considered the CV and focused less on projects than on papers. Low-quality papers could have a negative effect, while substantial awards also mattered.
  - Undergraduate grades were considered; graduate grades received less attention.
  - Interviews valued seriousness and attitude. If spoken English was unclear, candidates could politely ask the professor to repeat or rephrase rather than use a phone translator.
- Joint training did not award a doctoral degree, but could make a later postdoctoral position easier.
- The program offered substantial freedom to work according to personal interests and a supervisor's research.

## Miscellaneous Notes

- In the United Kingdom, programs could start in January, March, or September, with no single opening period, but scholarships had application deadlines—for example, January 13 for CSC and late December for a president's scholarship. An unconditional offer, including satisfaction of the language condition, was required before those deadlines.
- UK PhD applicants had to meet the language requirement in advance; the GRE was not required.
- CSC funding was difficult to obtain for the United States, and Australian visas were difficult to obtain because applications were not open. Few other destinations were considered better than Delft.
- Applicants whose undergraduate background was considered sensitive could have difficulty obtaining a German visa, especially in 5G-related fields.
- Among English-speaking countries, destinations other than the United Kingdom were difficult, including the United States and Canada. The Netherlands was described as relatively open; English was widely spoken in the Nordic countries, and southern Europe, including Italy and Spain, was also described as relatively open.
