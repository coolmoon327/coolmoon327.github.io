## Identifying Key Points While Reading

### Three Elements of a Sentence's Core Structure

1. Who is the sentence's **main subject**?
2. **With whom** does the subject have **what relationship**?
3. What **result follows from that relationship**?

Summarize a sentence in fewer than ten words in a mind map.

### Categories of Difficult Sentences

1. Too many clauses: split at punctuation and subordinate-clause signal words
   - If an unfamiliar word remains, then inspect the clause
2. Parenthetical material: skip insertions that merely supplement the main subject
   - Material between two commas or two dashes can often be skipped unless the main clause is unclear
3. Unfamiliar American-English words: replace rare words with abbreviations
4. Subjunctive inversion: restore normal word order; inversion moves an element forward for emphasis

### Signal Words

- Like: a paragraph contains a tenor and a vehicle, each of which may be explained by separate sentences
  - Represent them as parallel branches in the mind map
- Little, few: almost none
  - They are often contrasted with material indicating a change

## Paragraph Structure

- Main idea of the paragraph
- Main-idea signals: transition, contrast, summary, emphasis, cause and effect, definition, question and answer, sequence
- Supporting details

  - Repetition: two sentences often express the same idea, with identical subjects and objects but differently worded relationship terms
  - Explanation: a later sentence often supports or expands the preceding one

## Overall Passage Structure

1. Classification
2. Comparison/Contrast
3. Problem/Solution
4. Cause/Effect

## Answering Questions

> Take notes from which you can retell the original passage. The notes should be less than one-tenth the length of the source.

### Unfamiliar Words

1. Context: the second half of a sentence often explains the word
2. Logical signals: for a **proper noun**, connect it directly to the sentence's logical point
3. Infer from roots and affixes, much like recognizing part of a Chinese character
4. Replace it with its initial: use the word's first letter as a mental placeholder
